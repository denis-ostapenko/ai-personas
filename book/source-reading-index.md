## FN02: 9

Temporal databases have long distinguished valid time, when something holds in the represented world, from transaction time, when it is recorded in the database. Richard Snodgrass develops that distinction systematically in *Developing Time-Oriented Database Applications in SQL*. A record can be accurate about one time and misleading about the other. These are established data-management concepts, although their application to a conversational persona requires additional decisions about evidence and interpretation. [FN02]

## AM09: 9

The STALE benchmark studies this problem as implicit conflict. Its May 7, 2026 preprint separates recognizing an outdated state, resisting questions that assume the old state, and adapting later behavior to the new state. Its constructed scenarios and automated evaluation provide a diagnostic setting, rather than a population estimate of failure in everyday assistants. [AM09]

## AM05: 10

## R4B03: 10

Nelson Liu and colleagues' *Lost in the Middle* used controlled changes to the position of relevant information in multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval. The models tested often performed better when the relevant information appeared near the beginning or end than when it appeared in the middle. The study's model versions and tasks belong to its 2023 research setting; its lasting contribution is a testable positional failure mode, not a prediction that every later model will show the same curve.
